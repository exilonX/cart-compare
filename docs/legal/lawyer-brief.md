# Brief for a Romanian IT lawyer

Prepared 9 Nov 2026 for [Get a Romanian IT lawyer to confirm our collection and publication approach](https://github.com/exilonX/cart-compare/issues/23). It is a list of questions, not legal analysis.

## The approach to review

The Grocery Basket Index is a civic, open-source, not-for-profit weekly index of grocery Basket prices in Bucharest. One volunteer runs it, with no legal entity, no income and no funders.

- **Collection.** A person visits one named store of each of Lidl, Kaufland, Penny and Mega Image once a week. They note the shelf prices of about 25–30 items, as any shopper sees them, plus member (loyalty-card) prices where the shelf label or the Chain's public leaflet shows a price for all members. No accounts, cards, apps or websites are used. Photos of labels are taken only where house rules allow, and kept privately as evidence.
- **Not used:** Monitorul Prețurilor, Chain websites, any data feed, any request for permission.
- **Publication.** A weekly web page with:
  - Basket totals per Chain, Shelf and Loyalty side by side;
  - the price of every item as recorded;
  - item specifications, store names, code and a changelog;
  - a shareable image card with Chain names in plain text and no logos.

  Data is published under CC BY 4.0 and code under MIT. A Chain is named "cheapest" only if it leads by at least 2.5%, otherwise a tie is shown. A claims policy (no badges, citation conditions) is set by us alone. There is a public corrections log, and a Chain's reply is published next to the result when it writes to us. No notices are sent to Chains before publication.
- **Stop on notice.** Any activity a Chain or an authority objects to in writing is paused while the lawyer reviews the objection.
- **Not in the MVP:** discount checks, cross-country comparisons, Monitorul Prețurilor, website collection, Loyalty prices from accounts.

The full decisions are on the [map](https://github.com/exilonX/cart-compare/issues/1).

## What the go/no-go needs from you

The project's Legal kill criterion is tripped if any of these holds:

- criminal exposure remains;
- you cannot say that civil claims against the approach above are **unlikely to succeed**;
- the Core cannot survive Stop on notice.

Questions 1–6 decide that criterion. Questions 7–12 shape the design.

## Questions

**Collecting prices in store**

1. May a member of the public note shelf prices in a store open to the public, by hand or on a phone, in order to publish them? Can a store's house rules forbid this, and what follows if staff ask the person to stop or leave?
2. Photographing shelf labels: what limits do house rules and the image rights of people who appear in shot impose, if the photos are kept privately as evidence and never published?

**Publishing (decisive)**

3. If a Chain claims harm to its reputation (Civil Code art. 72, 253, 257, 1357) over a dated, method-backed, verifiable ranking published in good faith (art. 75), is the claim unlikely to succeed? What would make it likely to succeed?
4. Does Law 11/1991 (unfair competition) or Law 158/2008 (comparative advertising) reach an individual publishing a non-commercial civic index? Which facts would bring the project within them: donations, grants, a maintainer employed in grocery retail?
5. As an individual with no legal entity, what is the maintainer's personal exposure? Which form (an association under OG 26/2000, a foundation, or staying an individual) limits it, and is media-liability insurance available for such a project?
6. Is there any criminal exposure in the approach above?

**Design**

7. May Chain names be used in plain text to identify them (Law 84/1998 art. 45(1)(c), EUTMR art. 14(1)(c))?
8. May member prices read from shelf labels or public leaflets be published without restriction?
9. Can CC BY 4.0 validly cover a dataset of prices we observed ourselves, and what disclaimer should accompany it?
10. If a Chain cites our ranking in its advertising, does liability stay with the advertiser (Law 158/2008 art. 17)? Can the claims policy be made binding on Chains through the data licence?
11. Is a unilateral claims policy safe under competition law (Law 21/1996 art. 5), given that we never consult the Chains on it?
12. Has Romania transposed the anti-SLAPP Directive 2024/1069, and does it reach purely domestic cases?

## Dropped from the original lists

Questions about Monitorul Prețurilor, Chain website terms, database rights in Chains' websites, text and data mining, logged-in accounts, the discount check and foreign data sources are dropped. The approach no longer uses any of them. The original lists are in the [collection note](https://github.com/exilonX/cart-compare/blob/research/legal-collection/docs/research/legal-collection.md#questions-for-a-romanian-it-lawyer) and the [publication note](https://github.com/exilonX/cart-compare/blob/research/legal-publication/docs/research/legal-publication.md#questions-for-a-romanian-it-lawyer).
