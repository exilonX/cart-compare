# Who could host or fund a civic grocery price index in Romania for three years?

Ticket [#19](https://github.com/exilonX/cart-compare/issues/19), part of map [#1](https://github.com/exilonX/cart-compare/issues/1). Desk research, written 2026-10-21. Every figure carries the date its source gives. Terms follow the project glossary (`CONTEXT.md`): Comparable, Social value, Basket, Chain.

This note builds on earlier research and does not repeat it: [western Comparables (#4)](https://github.com/exilonX/cart-compare/blob/research/western-comparables/docs/research/western-comparables.md) (how Which?, OCU, DECO and CHOICE are funded), [Romanian shoppers](https://github.com/exilonX/cart-compare/blob/research/romanian-shoppers/docs/research/romanian-shoppers.md) (candidate partners: APC România, InfoCons, ICCV, journalists) and [legal publication](https://github.com/exilonX/cart-compare/blob/research/legal-publication/docs/research/legal-publication.md) (money from a Chain or supplier would likely bring the project within unfair-competition and comparative-advertising law).

**Source labels** used after each important claim, strongest first:

- **[Funder]**: the funder's or programme operator's own call page, guidelines or official release.
- **[Org]**: the organisation's own website or annual report.
- **[Media]**: a news report.
- **[Prior]**: an earlier research note in this repo, which cites its own primary sources.
- **[Calc]** or **[Assumption]**: my own arithmetic, or an assumption I state openly.

Currency conversions use ECB reference rates for 20 October 2026: €1 = 5.3308 lei = A$1.6056 = £0.84028 ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/policy_and_exchange_rates/euro_reference_exchange_rates/html/index.en.html)). [Funder]

## Answer

- **No Romanian organisation runs or funds a weekly grocery Basket index today, and none has a published process for adopting an outside project.** The plausible hosts are a civic-tech NGO (Code for Romania), the two consumer NGOs (APC România, InfoCons), an academic partner for the Basket method (ICCV), and newsrooms for distribution. Each would need a direct approach. [Org]
- **Code for Romania is the closest fit as an operator, but it builds from its own research agenda.** It says it maintains more than 70 open-source civic tools and takes "full responsibility" for them. Its 2025 expenses were 6.29m lei (about €1.18m), and private sponsorship was 54% of its revenue ([2025 annual report](https://d31wjq819xkxeg.cloudfront.net/Raport-2025-nou-compressed.pdf)). Its tools come out of its own Civic Labs research programme, and its sponsors are not listed in text, so they could not be checked against the no-Chain-money rule. [Org]
- **ICCV fits the Basket method, not daily operations.** ICCV has published normative consumption Baskets for decent living and subsistence since at least 2010. An ICCV researcher co-wrote the 2018 decent-living Basket published by Friedrich-Ebert-Stiftung Romania ([Mihăilescu 2010](https://ces.ro/newlib/studii-ces/ICCV_Cosul-de-consum.pdf); [FES catalogue, Sept 2018](https://collections.fes.de/publikationen/ident/fes/14759)). [Org]
- **The consumer NGOs do not publish current finances online.** APC România's latest annual report online is for 2013 ([APC reports page](https://www.apc-romania.ro/ro/pg-rapoarte-anuale/MTUtMS02.html)). InfoCons shows a "Marca Încrederii InfoCons" trust mark but does not say how it is awarded or whether companies pay for it ([infocons.ro](https://infocons.ro/)). Their independence from Chains cannot be checked without asking them. [Org]
- **The largest funding source within reach is the new EEA Civil Society Fund for Romania (€53.8m, launched 2 July 2026).** No call had been published as of 2026-10-21. Grants run until 30 April 2031. Its priorities include civic engagement, "information integrity" and participation in democratic processes; consumer protection is not named ([FDSC, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.fdsc.ro/en/eea-civil-society-fund-launches-in-romania/)). The same FDSC-led consortium runs the Swiss Civic Engagement Programme (CHF 18m, 2025–2028; grants of CHF 50,000–200,000 in its second window) ([FDSC](https://www.fdsc.ro/en/the-civic-engagement-programme-launches-its-second-funding-window-grants-for-projects-with-local-or-national-impact/)). [Funder]
- **EU-level grants are closed for 2026 or fit poorly.**
  - CERV citizens' engagement closed on 29 April 2026. Grants start at €75,000 and most priorities need a consortium from two countries ([Cyprus funding portal](https://www.fundingprogrammesportal.gov.cy/en/call/citizens-engagement-and-participation-en-2026/)).
  - Creative Europe Journalism Partnerships closed on 4 February 2026. It gives up to €2m per project at 80% co-funding and needs three partners in three countries ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/funding/call-proposals-journalism-partnerships-collaborations-2026)).
  - NLnet's open calls (€5,000–50,000, deadline 3 November 2026) fund internet infrastructure, not end-user civic sites ([NLnet Restack](https://nlnet.nl/restack/)).
  - The EU consumer-programme call was open only to dispute-resolution bodies and "qualified entities" ([SMP-CONS-2026 call document](https://www.euro-access.eu/_media/file/1053_call-fiche_smp-cons-2026-adr-rad_en.pdf)).

  [Funder]
- **Private foundations offer little today.**
  - No open Romanian foundation call that fits was found.
  - The Vodafone Foundation excludes projects that already have another institutional funder ([Fundația Vodafone](https://fundatia-vodafone.ro/finantare/)).
  - Civitates lists only past calls ([Civitates](https://civitates-eu.org/funding-opportunity/)).
  - Kaufland runs its own €600,000 NGO grant programme, Start ONG 2026 ([StartupCafe, 18 Mar 2026](https://startupcafe.ro/kaufland-finantari-totale-600000-euro-proiecte-civice-romania-96429)). The project's no-Chain-money policy excludes it.

  [Funder, Media]
- **Reader money can sustain Romanian civic media.** Recorder reported 2025 income of €3.62m, 90% of it from about 50,000 donors ([StartupCafe](https://startupcafe.ro/jurnalisti-recorder-venituri-milioane-euro-anul-2025-raport-financiar-100659)). Romania's yearly 3.5% income-tax redirect (deadline 25 May in 2026) is another recurring source, but it needs a Romanian non-profit to receive it ([StartupCafe, 22 May 2026](https://startupcafe.ro/redirectionarea-a-35-din-impozit-catre-ong-luni-25-mai-termen-pentru-depunerea-formularului-230-100163)). [Media]
- **Rough three-year cost** (assumptions are in the cost section):
  - about €5,000–20,000 for a volunteer-run index;
  - about €140,000–290,000 for a lean paid team at Romanian pay levels of August 2026;
  - about €0.5m–0.7m for a CHOICE-scale index with fieldwork. CHOICE's three-year grant of A$1.1m is about €0.69m at today's rate.

  [Calc]

### Romanian pay levels used

- **Average pay.** In August 2026 average gross monthly earnings were 9,497 lei and average net earnings 5,688 lei. Net earnings were 5.6% higher than a year earlier ([INS release no. 262, 13 Oct 2026](https://insse.ro/cms/sites/default/files/com_presa/com_pdf/cs08r26.pdf)). [Funder]
- **IT pay.** "Activități de programare și activități de consultanță în tehnologia informației" (IT programming and consulting) had the highest average net earnings, at 13,010 lei (same release). [Funder]
- **Employer cost per full-time person** [Calc, Assumption]. Net pay is taken as about 58.5% of gross: the employee pays 25% pension and 10% health contributions, then 10% income tax on the rest. The employer adds 2.25%. On that basis:
  - developer at IT pay: about 22,740 lei a month, or **≈ €51,200 a year**;
  - editor or methodologist at average pay: about 9,711 lei a month, or **≈ €21,900 a year**.

Domain, design and incidentals cost €1,000–3,000 a year [Assumption]. | **≈ €5,000–20,000** |

The brief's success signal is eight gap-free weeks. Tier A can probably reach that. Three years without gaps is where the volunteer Comparables failed: Heisse Preise lost 9 store feeds from February 2024 onward, and Trolley nearly closed over one bill ([Prior](https://github.com/exilonX/cart-compare/blob/research/western-comparables/docs/research/western-comparables.md)). Tier B is the smallest budget that pays someone to fix a broken collector every week.

## Gaps and uncertainties

- **EEA Civil Society Fund details.** Grant sizes, co-financing, eligibility (for example, whether a newly registered association can apply) and call dates were not published as of 2026-10-21. FDSC's home page returned no readable news.
- **Swiss Civic Engagement Programme.** The window-2 deadline and whether further windows are planned were not found.
- **CERV Union values call.** The dates (27 Oct 2026 to 24 Feb 2027) come only from a search summary of the Polish national contact point's schedule. The PDF refused connections. The 2027 CERV and Creative Europe calls were not checked.
- **Consumer NGOs' finances.** APC România and InfoCons publish no current financial statements or funder lists online. The terms of InfoCons's trust mark are unknown. Whether APC is a designated qualified entity for representative actions was not checked.
- **Code for Romania.** The sponsor list exists only as logos. No process for adopting an outside project is published. Whether any sponsor is a Chain or supplier was not checked.
- **ICCV.** Current capacity and interest are unknown. No ICCV Basket edition after 2018 was found. Romanian research-grant routes (UEFISCDI) were not checked.
- **Recorder figures** come from a media report of its financial statement, not the statement itself.
- **Cost model.** The legal-review cost and the overhead rates are assumptions. Employer cost uses standard contribution rates and ignores personal deductions and sector exceptions. Actual hiring costs vary.
- **Tax rules.** Whether a non-profit must be on ANAF's register to receive the 3.5% redirect or company sponsorship, and the 2026 sponsorship-deduction rules, were not verified. The 3.5% source quotes salary income only; one media report also lists other income types.
- **Not checked** (search budget): ERSTE Stiftung, Open Society Foundations, Google News Initiative, IJ4EU, community foundations, Lidl, Mega Image and Carrefour grant programmes, the 2026 status of the Vodafone Foundation's Connecting for Good, and whether any Romanian public body has ever funded a consumer group's price work, as Australia funds CHOICE.

## Questions this raises

1. **Legal home.** Should the project register its own Romanian association, use a fiscal host (Open Source Europe, 8–10% fee), or ask to be hosted by Code for Romania? Most Romanian grants and the 3.5% redirect need a Romanian non-profit.
2. **Who to approach first.** Should it be Code for Romania (operations), ICCV (Basket method, especially the Pensioner Basket), or both? Should APC România and InfoCons first be asked to disclose their funding and trust-mark terms?
3. **Funding policy wording.** The legal note already proposes refusing money from Chains, suppliers and "retail-adjacent" businesses. Does that also exclude Chain foundations (Kaufland's Start ONG) and intermediaries that administer Chain money? Are banks and telecoms acceptable?
4. **EEA fund framing.** Is the index best framed as "information integrity" or civic participation for the EEA Civil Society Fund? Should the team ask FDSC before the first call opens?
5. **A cross-border consortium.** CERV and Creative Europe need partners in 2–3 countries. Is it worth forming one with Bulgarian or Hungarian partners? This links to the country-comparison work and the CEE Comparables ticket.
6. **Which tier to plan for.** Should the MVP run as Tier A for 8 weeks to earn a track record, then apply for Tier B money? Or should it be costed as Tier B from the start for the EEA application?
7. **A public funder.** Would the project accept money from a Romanian public body (for example a ministry or the Competition Council, which already runs Monitorul Prețurilor), as Australia funds CHOICE? What would that do to perceived independence?
