# Who in Romania would use a basket index, and how would they find it?

Research ticket [#7](https://github.com/exilonX/cart-compare/issues/7) on the map [#1](https://github.com/exilonX/cart-compare/issues/1). Desk research, written 2026-10-21. It looks at social usefulness, not technical feasibility. Terms follow `CONTEXT.md`: Basket, Chain, Shelf price, Loyalty price, Comparable, Demand signal, Social value.

**Evidence tiers** (strongest first), shown in brackets after each important claim:

- **T1**: academic study. None was found in this pass; see Gaps.
- **T2**: official statistics or regulator data (INS, Eurostat).
- **T3**: industry survey (NielsenIQ, Shopfully/iSense, Reuters Institute Digital News Report, DataReportal).
- **T4**: media report, a Chain's own published figure, or a third-party estimate (Ahrefs traffic, Google Play install bands).

## Answer

- **The need is broad and measurable.**
  - Food and non-alcoholic drinks take **32.0% of household consumption spending** (Q2 2026). The share is 34.2% for pensioner households and 35.5% for rural households (2025) [T2].
  - Romania gives food the largest weight in the EU's inflation basket: 27.4% against an EU average of 16.3% (2026) [T2].
  - 18.1% of Romanians cannot afford a protein meal every second day. Among people aged 65+ who live alone the figure is 29.9% (EU: 8.5% and 12.2%, 2025) [T2].
  - The Overview's line "food takes more than a third of household income" should read "about a third of household *consumption spending*". Food is about 15.5% of total income.
- **Romanians already compare and switch, but among nearby stores.**
  - Promotions were 27.7% of modern-trade sales in Q2 2026. NielsenIQ says shoppers buy "less but more often and from more stores" [T3].
  - 60% say they split their shopping across several stores, and 69% would change Chain for promotions. Yet 70% shop mainly at the nearest or most convenient store [T3].
  - So the index is most useful for choosing among the 2–4 Chains a person can reach, and for checking whether a promotion is real.
- **The most reachable segment is working-age, budget-minded households (25–54), especially families.**
  - They are mobile-first: 96.5% use the internet on a smartphone and 85.9% use social networks [T2].
  - They already look for deals digitally: 4 in 10 Romanians follow promotions through catalogue apps or sites [T3].
  - Channel: shareable weekly cards on Facebook (10.3m ad reach), YouTube and TikTok [T3], amplified by press pick-up.
- **Journalists are the fastest multiplier, and they are already asking for this.**
  - Outlets publish "most expensive supermarket" rankings without a stated method ([Playtech, Sept 2025](https://playtech.ro/2025/cele-mai-scumpe-supermarketuri-din-romania-in-2025-surpriza-la-locul-al-doilea/); [Puterea, Apr 2025](https://www.puterea.ro/?p=483277)).
  - Economedia's May 2026 price-hunting guide tells readers to build their own fixed list of 15–25 staples and rotate stores ([Economedia](https://economedia.ro/?p=350054)) [T4]. That is a Basket index done by hand.
  - A documented, citable weekly index fills a gap the press already shows.
- **Trust comes from method, not brand.**
  - Trust in news is 23%, a record low, and only 2 in 10 online Romanians trust information on social platforms [T3].
  - TV is still the main news source for 58% of online Romanians. Pro TV reaches 60% weekly and has been the most trusted news brand for ten years [T3].
  - Published item lists and matching rules (already in the Overview) are the index's main trust asset.
- **Pensioners have the greatest need and are the hardest to reach directly.**
  - There are 4.91m pensioners, with an average pension of 2,942 lei a month (Q2 2026) [T2].
  - Among people aged 65–74, 76.6% use the internet on a smartphone and 54.2% use social networks. But only 22.4% look up information about goods online, 7.3% have basic digital skills and 8.3% bank online [T2]. No such data exists for people aged 75+.
  - Older shoppers lean on printed leaflets [T3].
  - Reach them through TV, radio and press stories built on the Pensioner Basket, and through their adult children. Do not count on them finding a website.
- **Students and single young people are the easiest to reach digitally, but the weakest-evidenced need.**
  - Among 16–24s, 97.3% use the internet on a smartphone and 93.6% use social networks [T2].
  - No Romanian data was found on their grocery spending or price-hunting. Treat them as a secondary audience.
- **Loyalty schemes are mainstream, so the Loyalty price matters.** These are the Chains' own figures [T4], measured in different ways and not comparable:
  - PENNY Card: 4.2m card holders at end-2025.
  - Carrefour app: over 3m users (June 2025).
  - Auchan MyCLUB: over 2m active members, used in over 60% of daily purchases (March 2026).
  - Kaufland: 70% of card use is digital (Sept 2024).
  - Lidl says Lidl Plus is Romania's most used food-retail loyalty app.
  - The Overview's plan to show both the Shelf price and the Loyalty price is justified.
- **Demand signals are real but fragmented.**
  - Leaflet aggregators (Kimbino, Catalomat, Blix, Ofertelino) and comparators (RadarPrețuri) exist [T4].
  - The largest aggregator draws an estimated 110–150k organic visits a month, against about 0.8m for lidl.ro [T4]. Shoppers mostly go straight to the Chains' own leaflets and apps.
  - 55% of Romanians say a tool that compares prices across retailers would be useful [T3].

Two ownership facts matter for how the index presents "8 Chains":

- **Profi and Mega Image now have the same owner.** Capital reports that Profi Rom Food "a fost absorbită anul acesta de Mega Image" ("was absorbed this year by Mega Image"), part of Ahold Delhaize ([Capital, 15 July 2026](https://www.capital.ro/surpriza-din-topul-supermarketurilor-din-romania-cine-a-avut-cele-mai-mari-profituri-in-2025.html); [Capital, 2 June 2026](https://www.capital.ro/batalia-gigantilor-in-retail-devine-tot-mai-stransa-lidl-este-lider-de-piata-dar-mega-image-si-profi-castiga-teren.html)) [T4].
- **Carrefour Romania changed owner.** It "a trecut recent sub controlul proprietarilor Dedeman" ("recently passed under the control of Dedeman's owners") ([Capital, 2 June 2026](https://www.capital.ro/batalia-gigantilor-in-retail-devine-tot-mai-stransa-lidl-este-lider-de-piata-dar-mega-image-si-profi-castiga-teren.html)) [T4].

**Channels.** NielsenIQ's H1 2026 retail barometer measured about 62,000 points of sale [T3, via [Wall-Street.ro/Agerpres, 17 Aug 2026](https://www.wall-street.ro/articol/retail/romanii-cheltuiesc-68-mld-lei-pe-fmcg-si-electro-it.html)]:

- Supermarkets and discounters hold 49.5% of FMCG value and grew 8.9%.
- Hypermarkets hold 19.8%.
- **Traditional trade still holds 29.7%.** Almost a third of grocery spending happens outside the Chains the index covers.
- Private label is 21.9% of FMCG sales.
- Online buying of food from stores is small: 5.0% of Romanians did it in the three months before the 2024 survey, against 11.0% in the EU. Among people aged 65–74 the figure is 1.4% ([Eurostat isoc_ec_ibgs](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/isoc_ec_ibgs/default/table)) [T2].

**Switching and promotions.**

- **NielsenIQ, H1 2026** [T3]. Promotions rose to 27.7% of modern-trade sales in Q2 2026, up 1.4 points on a year earlier. The release says "Vânzările promoţionale au reprezentat 27,7% din totalul vânzărilor reţelelor de comerţ modern" ("Promotional sales made up 27.7% of modern-trade chain sales"). It describes shoppers "cumpărând mai puţin dar mai frecvent şi din mai multe magazine" ("buying less but more often and from more stores"). NIQ links this to purchasing power about 6% lower than in 2025: average inflation was 9.4% against wage growth of 3.2% ([Wall-Street.ro](https://www.wall-street.ro/articol/retail/romanii-cheltuiesc-68-mld-lei-pe-fmcg-si-electro-it.html)).
- **NielsenIQ, autumn 2025** [T3]. Promotions were 25.3% of modern-trade sales in the first nine months of 2025: "Românii au devenit vânători de chilipiruri" ("Romanians have become bargain hunters") ([TVR Info, 4 Nov 2025](https://tvrinfo.ro/?p=651837)).
- **NielsenIQ Shopper Trends 2025** [T3, via a secondary summary]. This was presented in May 2025 and surveyed 1,600 urban consumers ([Arta Alba, 24 June 2025](https://artaalba.ro/en/consumer-profile-nielseniq-study/)):
  - The average urban shopper visits **3.3 stores a month**.
  - About 43% choose a store because of its promotions.
  - Over 50% say their income covers only basic needs.
  - For information about offers, "mobile apps dominate, followed by printed flyers"; television is losing relevance.
- **Shopfully, "The State of Shopping 2026"** [T3]. Fieldwork was in Q1 2026: 6,605 adults aged 25+ in 8 countries, including Romania. The Romanian sample size was not published. Shopfully sells drive-to-store and leaflet advertising, so it has an interest in digital catalogues.
  - 60% split their purchases across several stores to find the best deals, second-highest of the 8 countries after Italy.
  - 39% say promotions influence them "extrem de mult" ("extremely"), against a 25% European average.
  - 67% prefer to shop mainly in physical stores.
  - Sources: [Revista Biz, 27 May 2026](https://www.revistabiz.ro/romanii-au-schimbat-comportamentul-la-cumparaturi-se-documenteaza-online-si-cauta-promotii/); [Observator, 28 May 2026](https://observatornews.ro/economic/inflatia-schimba-comportamentul-de-consum-vaneaza-oferte-si-cauta-produsele-online-inainte-de-a-le-cumpara-655866.html).
  - A second release from the same study says "69% sunt dispuși să schimbe lanțurile de magazine pe care le frecventează de obicei în funcție de promoții" ("69% are willing to change the Chains they usually visit depending on promotions"). But 70% shop at the nearest or most convenient stores ([Revista Biz, 22 June 2026](https://www.revistabiz.ro/romanii-cauta-cele-mai-bune-promotii-si-sunt-dispusi-sa-schimbe-magazinul-obisnuit/)).

**Reading.** Switching is frequent but local. Romanians move between the Chains they can reach for promotions. Few cross the city for a cheaper Basket. Two questions are probably more actionable than a national "cheapest Chain" ranking: which nearby Chain is cheapest for my Basket this week, and is this promotion real?

What this means for the index:

- **Romania is mobile-only, not just mobile-first.** Smartphone use is above the EU average in every age group. But only 37% of Romanians also use a computer, against 64.5% in the EU, and only 9% of people aged 65–74 do. Every page and shareable card has to work on a phone first.
- **Digital skills are the lowest in the EU.** 31.8% have at least basic skills; Bulgaria is next lowest at 38.3%. Interfaces must be very simple.
- **Older Romanians are social but do not search.** People aged 65–74 use social networks more than their EU peers (54.2% vs 34.1%). But they look up product information online at less than half the EU rate (22.4% vs 51.0%). They can be reached *in the feed*, through Facebook posts and messages forwarded by family. They will not go looking for a price site.
- **No data covers people aged 75+.** Eurostat's survey stops at 74, and INS uses the same scope. Many pensioners are older than that.

## 5. Leaflet aggregators and deal communities as a Demand signal

**How people find offers.**

- 69% of Romanians research products online before buying. Of those, 49% use catalogue sites and apps that show store promotions, and 48% use retailer or brand sites and apps [T3, Shopfully 2026, [Revista Biz](https://www.revistabiz.ro/romanii-au-schimbat-comportamentul-la-cumparaturi-se-documenteaza-online-si-cauta-promotii/)].
- An iSense Solutions study for Shopfully found that "Patru din zece români caută și urmăresc promoțiile direct prin intermediul aplicațiilor" ("four in ten Romanians look for and follow promotions directly through apps") with digital catalogues [T3, [Revista Biz, 22 Oct 2025](https://www.revistabiz.ro/4-din-10-romani-urmaresc-promotiile-online-ale-brandurilor-sau-retailerilor/)]. People aged 35–44 prefer digital catalogues, while "publicul mai în vârstă este predispus să consulte cataloagele tipărite" ("the older public tends to consult printed catalogues"). Women follow promotions most often, many of them weekly. No sample size was published.

**Tools Romanians are pointed to.** Economedia's May 2026 guide "Cum să identifici cele mai mici prețuri la alimente" ("How to find the lowest food prices") lists these [T4, [Economedia, 27 May 2026](https://economedia.ro/?p=350054)]:

- Chains' weekly online catalogues, and supermarket apps with personalised discounts.
- Monitorul Prețurilor. The guide notes it "nu include secțiune separată dedicată cataloagelor săptămânale" ("has no section for weekly catalogues").
- RadarPrețuri.ro, a comparator that collects prices automatically and may lag behind the shelf.
- Blix and Ofertelino, catalogue aggregators. Ofertelino "nu colectează prețuri individuale din magazine" ("does not collect individual store prices").
- Listiq, a shopping-list app that "nu compară automat prețuri între magazine" ("does not compare prices between stores automatically").
- A method rather than a tool: "crearea unei liste fixe de 15 – 25 de produse de bază" ("create a fixed list of 15–25 staples"), then rotate stores to find the cheapest overall Basket.

The press is already teaching readers to build a Basket index by hand, because no tool does it.

**Reading.** The appetite for deal-hunting is clear and widespread. The current tools find promotions but do not compare Baskets. The Chains' own leaflets and apps dominate. That is the gap: an index that turns promotions into a weekly Basket total. It is also a risk. The index competes for attention with the Chains' own apps, which shoppers already open every week.

## 6. Media and channels Romanians trust for consumer information

**Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026, Romania page** (author Raluca-Nicoleta Radu, University of Bucharest; 16 June 2026) [T3]. The survey is online, so it describes internet users and under-represents offline older people ([DNR 2026 Romania](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/romania)):

- Overall trust in news is **23%**, down 3 points, against a 37% global average: "Trust in news overall reached a new low point, for the fifth year in a row."
- Weekly reach, online and offline combined: **Pro TV 60%**, Antena 1 38%, Digi24 38%. "Pro TV has been the most trusted and most used news brand over the last ten years."
- The public broadcasters TVR and Radio România are "two of the five most trusted media brands but are much smaller in terms of reach."
- 32% get news content from news-focused creators.
- Recorder, eighth among online brands and mostly funded by donations, reached over 2m YouTube views in a day with one documentary.

Romanian coverage of the same report adds [T3, [Forbes România, 16 June 2026](https://www.forbes.ro/consumul-de-stiri-in-romania-televiziunile-rezista-dar-platformele-sociale-devin-principalul-canal-de-informare-506544)]:

- "televiziunea rămâne principala sursă de știri pentru 58%" ("television remains the main news source for 58%").
- For the first time, social and video platforms overtook TV and news sites as the main way to reach news.
- "doar două persoane din zece declară că au încredere în informațiile de pe platformele sociale" ("only two people in ten say they trust information on social platforms").
- TVR reached 17% weekly and Radio România 13%.

**Platform reach** (DataReportal "Digital 2026: Romania", Nov 2025, based on platforms' advertising tools; ad reach is not the same as active users) [T3, [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-romania)]:

| Platform | Ad reach |
| --- | --- |
| YouTube | 12.9m (68.3% of the population) |

| TikTok | 8.71m adults (57.0% of adults) |
| Messenger | 7.3m |

**Reading.**

- **Channels to reach people:** Facebook and YouTube for adults, TikTok for younger adults, TV for everyone (for older people above all).
- **Source of trust:** with trust in news and in social platforms both low, credibility has to come from method: published item lists, matching rules and a dated price history. It cannot come from a media brand or influencers.
- **TV as amplifier:** the most trusted, highest-reach brand is a TV channel. A Basket index that gives TV a weekly or monthly story ("the pensioner Basket rose X lei this month; it is Y lei cheaper at Chain Z") reaches the people least likely to visit the site.

## Gaps and uncertainties

- **The shared web-search budget ran out partway through.** The rest of the research used direct fetches of known primary pages and official data APIs. Not covered as a result:
  - Eurobarometer trust in TV, press, internet and social networks for Romania.
  - The EU Consumer Conditions Scoreboard: trust in consumer organisations and retailers.
  - Membership of Romanian Facebook and Reddit deal groups.
  - Monitorul Prețurilor traffic figures.
  - A search for Romanian academic studies.
  A follow-up pass should fill these.
- **No academic (T1) evidence was found** on how Romanians use price-comparison tools.
- **People aged 75+ are invisible.** Eurostat and INS ICT data stop at 74. DNR's online survey under-represents offline older people.
- **Loyalty figures are not comparable.** They are the Chains' own figures, with different definitions and dates. There are none for Lidl Plus in Romania, for Kaufland members or for Selgros, and Mega Image's figure dates from 2018. Google Play installs count downloads, not active users.
- **Commercial surveys have gaps.** Shopfully and iSense did not publish Romanian sample sizes, and Shopfully sells leaflet advertising. NielsenIQ Shopper Trends 2025 covered urban shoppers only and was read through a secondary summary.
- **Turnover figures are second-hand.** They come from press reports of statutory filings, not the filings themselves. Auchan's and Selgros's 2025 figures are missing.
- **About 30% of FMCG spending is outside the index.** Traditional trade (independent shops, markets) holds 29.7% [T3]. Pensioners and rural households may rely on it more; this was not checked.
- **Food shares include home-grown food** that households eat themselves, which flatters the food share of rural and pensioner households compared with what they spend in shops.
- **State food cards for pensioners.** About 2.6m beneficiaries in 2024; 250 lei a year in 2025 ([Capital](https://www.capital.ro/se-taie-banii-ce-suma-vor-primi-pensionarii-pe-cardurile-de-alimente-in-2025.html)) [T4]. Not checked against an official source, and which Chains accept them was not established.

**What only talking to people can answer:**

- Do pensioners want a weekly Basket price at all? Would they hear about it through TV, a grandchild's phone, or a printed sheet?
- Do families trust a civic index more than a Chain's own leaflet? Would they change Chain for a Basket saving of a given size, given that 70% shop at the nearest store?
- Which of the three questions (cheapest Chain this week, is this discount real, how expensive is life here) matters most to each segment?
- Do people price their shop at the Shelf price or the Loyalty price? How many cards and apps do they actually use?
- Would journalists cite a non-official index? What would they need: a method note, raw data, a named contact, a regular release day?

## Questions this raises

1. **Fix the Overview.** "Food takes more than a third of household income" should read "about a third of household consumption spending (32.0%, Q2 2026)". It should cite INS directly.
2. **Distribution before features.** Should the MVP be built around a regular press release and shareable cards (mobile-first, Facebook- and WhatsApp-ready), with the website as the place to check the method?
3. **The Pensioner Basket as a media product.** Should it be published mainly as a monthly story for TV and newspapers, perhaps with a consumer NGO, rather than as an interactive feature pensioners must find?
4. **Local, not national.** Given 3.3 stores a month and 70% shopping nearby, should results be framed around Chains and formats present in a neighbourhood or city? Is "cheapest Chain nationally" misleading for someone with only two Chains nearby?
5. **Headline price.** Which price leads the ranking? At Auchan most purchases use the card, but 43% of Romanians are wary of sharing data with stores.
6. **Counting Chains.** Profi and Mega Image now have one owner. Keep them as two Chains (different formats and prices) or group them?
7. **Traditional trade.** How should the index state that about 30% of grocery spending (independent shops, markets) is outside it?
8. **Partnership order.** Is the first partner a newsroom (reach), a consumer NGO (credibility with pensioners), or an academic institute such as ICCV (Basket method)? This feeds the map's "Partnerships" item.
9. **Interview guide inputs.** For the map's "Talking to Romanians" item:
   - Ask families: how many Chains they visit, how they find offers, which apps they use, whether they would change Chain for a given saving, whether "minutes of work" means anything to them.
   - Ask pensioners: which source they trust for prices, whether anyone shows them offers on a phone, whether they use printed leaflets, whether they buy at markets.
   - Ask a journalist: what makes a price comparison citable.
   - Ask an NGO: whether they would co-publish, and on what terms.
